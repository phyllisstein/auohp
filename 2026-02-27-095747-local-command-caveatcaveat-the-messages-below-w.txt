⏺ Good data. Here's what the graph actually looks like by the numbers, and what
  it means for your two questions.

  ---
  1. Are Person and Speaker supernodes?

  No. Not even close. A supernode is typically a node with hundreds of thousands
   or millions of relationships — think "USA" in a geographic graph or "English"
   in a language graph. Your numbers:

  ┌───────────────────────────────┬────────────┬────────────────┐
  │             Node              │ Max degree │  Relationship  │
  ├───────────────────────────────┼────────────┼────────────────┤
  │ Person (Jim Hubbard)          │ 6          │ INTERVIEWED_AS │
  ├───────────────────────────────┼────────────┼────────────────┤
  │ Person (Sarah Schulman)       │ 6          │ INTERVIEWED_AS │
  ├───────────────────────────────┼────────────┼────────────────┤
  │ Person (Vincent Gagliostro)   │ 1          │ INTERVIEWED_AS │
  ├───────────────────────────────┼────────────┼────────────────┤
  │ Speaker (largest interviewee) │ 186        │ SAYS           │
  ├───────────────────────────────┼────────────┼────────────────┤
  │ Transcript (Ashes Action)     │ 240        │ TRANSCRIBES    │
  └───────────────────────────────┴────────────┴────────────────┘

  Even if you seed all 187 AUOHP interviews, Jim and Sarah would each have ~187
  INTERVIEWED_AS edges. That's still trivial. The largest Speaker would still
  top out around 200 SAYS edges per transcript. Neo4j handles these without
  breaking a sweat.

  The thing that could become a problem isn't degree — it's label scans. Your
  search query in use-neo4j-transcript.ts starts from a vector index hit on
  :Statement, then walks the graph outward. That's fine. But if you ever wrote a
   query like MATCH (p:Person {name: $name}) without an index, that's a label
  scan — and it would be slow at scale not because Person is a supernode, but
  because it lacks an index. (You do have a fulltext index on Person.name, so
  you're covered there.)

  Verdict: Speaker and Person are not a problem. Don't refactor for scale 
  reasons.

  ---
  2. What is worth refactoring — and what would make this more graph-walkable?

  Here are the actual structural issues I see, ranked by impact:

  A. Statements have no temporal chain

  Right now, to get "the 5 statements after this search hit," you'd have to:
  MATCH (t:Transcript)-[r:TRANSCRIBES]->(s:Statement)
  WHERE r.startTime >= $hitTime
  ORDER BY r.startTime
  LIMIT 5

  That's a sort-and-filter, not a graph walk. If you added a linked list:

  (s1:Statement)-[:NEXT]->(s2:Statement)-[:NEXT]->(s3:Statement)

  Then context expansion becomes a simple traversal:
  MATCH (hit:Statement)-[:NEXT*1..5]->(context:Statement)

  This matters most for the search UI — when someone finds a statement about
  "Stop the Church," they almost certainly want to read the surrounding
  conversation. A NEXT chain makes that a O(k) traversal instead of a sort over
  all statements in the transcript.

  B. SAYS is overloaded

  Right now SAYS means two completely different things:
  1. (Speaker)-[:SAYS]->(Statement) — a person utters a statement
  2. (Statement)-[:SAYS]->(Caption) — a statement contains sub-caption chunks

  This is confusing and makes generic graph walks ambiguous. The 6,672 SAYS
  edges break down as roughly 692 speaker→statement + 5,980 statement→caption.
  The Caption children should use a different relationship type — something like
   :HAS_CAPTION or :CONTAINS.

  C. The Speaker node is actually a good pattern — but poorly named

  The Speaker node is doing the work of a Participation or Role node. It
  represents "Person X participating in Transcript Y as diarization label Z."
  This is a legitimate graph modeling pattern for n-ary relationships. The
  CLAUDE.md FIXME suggesting you collapse it to (Person)-[:SAYS]->(Statement)
  would actually lose information:

  - The diarization label (SPEAKER_01) is scoped to a single transcript — it has
   no meaning outside it
  - Jim Hubbard is SPEAKER_03 in one interview and SPEAKER_00 in another
  - The documentary has 21 speakers, most unidentified — without the Speaker
  node, you'd have orphaned statements with no person to attach to

  What you could improve:
  - Rename it to something like Participant or Role to clarify its purpose
  - Add a role property ('interviewer' / 'interviewee' / 'unknown') instead of
  relying on label-based inheritance (:Interviewer / :Interviewee)

  D. Jim and Sarah's statements are silently dropped

  The FILTER_INTERVIEWER_SPEAKER_HACK env var causes interviewer statements to
  be skipped during seeding. Jim and Sarah each have 6 Speaker nodes with zero 
  SAYS edges. Their questions are lost entirely. This means:

  - The graph can't reconstruct the full conversation flow
  - A NEXT chain would have gaps where interviewer turns should be
  - The search can never surface the questions that prompted interesting answers

  If you add a NEXT chain, you'll want to seed interviewer statements too
  (perhaps with a label or property to distinguish them for display filtering).

  E. What a more walkable model looks like

  Here's what I'd suggest, preserving what works and fixing what doesn't:

  (Interview)-[:HAS_TRANSCRIPT]->(Transcript)
  (Transcript)-[:TRANSCRIBES {startTime, endTime}]->(Statement)
  (Statement)-[:NEXT]->(Statement)                          // NEW: temporal
  chain
  (Statement)-[:CONTAINS]->(Caption)                        // RENAMED from SAYS
  (Transcript)-[:INCLUDES_SPEAKER]->(Speaker)               // keep as-is
  (Speaker)-[:SAYS]->(Statement)                            // keep, now
  unambiguous
  (Person)-[:INTERVIEWED_AS]->(Speaker)                     // keep as-is
  (Statement)-[:MENTIONS]->(Action|Person|Collective|...)   // knowledge graph
  edges

  The big wins:
  1. NEXT chain — enables context traversal from any search hit
  2. CONTAINS replaces SAYS on Statement→Caption — removes ambiguity
  3. Keep Speaker — it earns its place as a transcript-scoped participation node
  4. Seed interviewer statements — complete the conversation graph, filter at
  display time
